Review: an agent every time, a person when critical

An agent reviews every milestone, and a person reviews as well only when the change is critical or its blast radius is large.

Release gate: a person accepts the remaining risk, only when the change is critical.

In this chapter

  • Agent review is always on, for milestones and for unplanned work.
  • The reviewer checks the change against its clauses, not its style.
  • A person reviews as well only when the policy lists the change as critical, or the reviewer rates its blast radius as large. That is the release gate.
  • The person reads the Verification Record and the behavior that changed, not the whole diff.

What arrives

A milestone that has been built and verified arrives with the Verification Record computed for it.

The agent review

The reviewer is an agent with its own account. It can read everything, writes nothing but its review, and had no part in building the code or writing the checks. For every milestone and every piece of unplanned work, it asks:

  1. Does every behavior trace to a clause? Code that does something no clause asked for is a finding.
  2. Does the actual change in behavior match the predicted one? The reviewer compares the prediction in change.md with what the diff and the checks show, and explains each difference as a bug, a gap in the canon, or a wrong prediction.
  3. Are the design choices written down? A choice the agents made silently cannot be revisited.
  4. Is the evidence honest? Each check encodes its example, each has a recorded failure, and nothing in verify/ was weakened.
  5. How large is the blast radius? What can break if this is wrong, for whom, and can it be undone?

What it found

The predicted change was that a refund moves from review to automatic approval while the total refunded on the order stays under $100, and nothing else changes. The diff has one more change: the payout retry count, PAYOUT_RETRIES in src/refunds/service.py, went from 3 to 5. When the gateway loses a reply, the new build would retry the payout five times where the old one retried three. The builder had edited the payout code, which the design said would not be changed. No clause asks for it, and retries are exactly where a refund gets paid twice.

The reviewer sends it back, and the builder sets the count back to 3, in the same commit as the review; every check still passes. The finding stays in review.md, and anyone who wants more retries can raise it as a request at Collect.

When a person reviews too

A person reviews when the policy lists the change as critical, or when the reviewer rates its blast radius as large. The team’s policy lists these cases:

A person reviews when the change Why
touches a constraint (ENV) or an action that cannot be undone Someone must answer for it
adds or alters a tier 2 clause (safety, money) The cost of being wrong is high
changes code that touches the outside world (adapters, permissions) It is the code evidence covers least
makes the status of any clause worse Something was traded away
behaves differently from what was predicted, and the difference is kept Nobody agreed to it
has a blast radius the reviewer rates as large Many customers or clauses are exposed if it is wrong

This person-review is the release gate. When no case applies, the milestone goes to release on the agent review alone.

DEC-031 is a tier 2 clause and M2 moves money without review, so the reviewer marks a person as required, and the refunds owner reviews it.

What the person reads

The refunds owner reads the evidence and the change in behavior instead of the diff.

  • The Verification Record for the release candidate. DEC-031 is accepted: three checks, each seen to fail. INV-004 has a fourth check, on the automatic path. No other clause got worse: 4 accepted, 1 accepted, with stated risk.
  • Predicted against actual behavior. After the fix, they match.
  • The assurance case for INV-004, since money is involved: why a refund still cannot be paid twice when the gateway loses a reply.

The owner signs off, and the sign-off is recorded in review.md (signed off by: refunds owner, 2026-03-09) as a named person taking responsibility for the remaining risk.

The one kind of code a person is asked to read is code that touches the outside world. After the fix, M2 leaves the gateway adapter and the payout code as they were.

Why both

The reviewer reads everything on every change, but it can share the builder’s misreading of a clause, since both are models reading the same words. A person reads less and more slowly, but brings judgment and can be held to account. The arrangement gives every change complete coverage by the agent and directs people’s attention to the changes with the largest consequences.

Review has no level of automation to turn up or down. What a team adjusts over time is the policy’s list of what counts as critical: a cautious team starts with a longer list and shortens it on track record. Handing over decisions describes how.

What leaves

A reviewed milestone, signed off by its owner, goes on to Release.
