## What arrives

A one-page proposal: refunds under $100 are approved automatically. It reads clearly. It hides two decisions.

## A word nobody defined

Before drafting anything, the elicitor checks every noun in the proposal against `vocabulary.md`. One is missing. Under $100 of what: the order, or the refund?

It asks the product manager, with the two readings side by side. The answer goes into the vocabulary.

```text
Refund amount   The money returned by one refund. Not the order total.
```

An agent that guessed would have built one of the two readings, and nobody would have known which until a customer complained.

## An example exposes a choice

The elicitor writes examples for the proposed decision. The third one has no obvious answer.

> Given an order of $150, when the customer asks for $60 back, and later for another $60, then what?

Each refund is under $100. Together they are not. The proposal does not say.

The agent does not pick. It recommends, and says why. The team's `tenets.md` has a line for this: *a person reviews before money moves, when in doubt.* So the recommendation is that the limit counts everything refunded on the order. The product manager agrees.

The decision is recorded as a clause, with the example that fixes its meaning.

```markdown
id: DEC-031
kind: decision
traces: [REQ-012]
---
Refunds under $100 are approved automatically.

Satisfies:
- Delivered order of $40: approved without review.
- Two $60 refunds on one order: the second goes to review.
Violates:
- Order of $250: approved without review.
```

The risk team's request is now an example that a check will enforce.

## How it will be built

Who leads the design is each team's choice. This team's architect keeps the architecture and leaves detailed design to the agents.

The architect makes one call: the new rule belongs in the decision logic, and the code that pays out is not touched. The planner agent writes the rest of the design, and a **predicted change in behavior**:

> Refunds move from review to automatic approval while the total refunded on the order stays under $100. Nothing else changes.

Later, a program compares this prediction with what actually changed.

## How it will be proven

The plan for evidence is written now, before the code. Scientists register a hypothesis before an experiment for the same reason: a test written after the code can be shaped to fit it.

The verification lead and the verifier agent decide what the test must survive. The existing environment, VE-002, has a fake payment gateway. They add one fault to it: the gateway pays, and then times out. An automatic approval that retries carelessly would pay twice, and INV-004 forbids that.

## The agreement gate

A **gate** is a point where a person must act before work moves on. This one closes the station.

| Who | Approves |
|---|---|
| Product manager | The examples for DEC-031 |
| Constraint owner | That ENV-003, the payout limit, is untouched |
| Verification lead | The argument for INV-004, because it concerns money |

People approve examples. They do not approve code, and there is none yet.

## What leaves

The milestone folder now holds whole files in their final form.

```text
roadmap/M2-faster-small-refunds/
  change.md            status: agreed
  diff/canon/          DEC-031, the new vocabulary entry
  diff/blueprint/      the design, the evidence plan
  tasks/               three named tasks
```

Nothing in `spec/` has changed. These files become the specification only at [Release](../release/). First the milestone is [built](../build/).
