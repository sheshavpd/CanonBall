## A choice nobody made

The refunds team is drafting a decision: refunds under $100 are approved automatically. An example exposes a gap. A customer asks for two $60 refunds on one order. Each is under $100. Together they are not.

Nobody has decided this case. Left alone, an agent picks an answer, and may pick the other one next month. Told to ask, it still has nothing to recommend from.

Both answers can be defended. One pays the customer sooner. The other keeps a person in front of the larger sum. The agent lacks one fact: which of the two this team puts first.

## What a tenet is

A **tenet** is a short statement of which good thing wins when two conflict. It is written "X over Y". People write the tenets in `spec/canon/tenets.md`, and agents read the file before every task.

```markdown
# Tenets

In priority order. When two tenets conflict, the higher one wins.

1. A person's review over speed, when money moves.
   A person reviews before money moves, when in doubt.
2. A slow refund over a wrongly refused one.
   A wrongly refused refund is worse than a slow one.
3. A rule support can explain over a rule that approves more.
   A customer who is refused is told why in one sentence.
```

Keep the list to a handful, in priority order. A long ranking is one nobody can apply.

## Why not "values"

A value names a good thing nobody disagrees with: speed, safety, fairness to the customer. Every team would sign that list, so it tells an agent nothing. In the split-refund case speed and safety point in opposite directions, and the list is silent.

A tenet says which one wins. One test separates the two: could a sensible team have written the opposite? A shop selling low-priced goods might put speed over review. If no team would write the opposite, the statement is a value, and it settles nothing.

## How an agent uses one

Choices like this one surface at the [Shape station](../shape/), where a milestone's clauses are settled. The elicitor, the agent that drafts clauses, brings the question to the product manager with a recommendation and cites the tenet behind it.

```text
Question    Two $60 refunds on one order. Does the $100 limit
            apply to each refund, or to the total refunded
            on the order?
Recommend   The total refunded on the order.
Cites       Tenet 1. A person reviews before money moves,
            when in doubt.
```

The product manager decides, and here agrees. A tenet supports a recommendation. It never makes the decision.

## From a tenet to a decision

When a tenet settles a specific question, the answer is recorded as a [decision](../primitives/#decision) that cites it. Here the decision is DEC-031, "Refunds under $100 are approved automatically". It gains an example, "two $60 refunds on one order: the second goes to review", and names tenet 1 as its reason.

From then on the case is decided, and its example becomes a check.

A question that comes up twice with no tenet to settle it is a sign that one is missing.

## What a tenet is not

| | A clause | A tenet |
|---|---|---|
| Says | One thing the system is held to | Which good thing wins when two conflict |
| Has examples and checks | Yes | No |
| Appears in the Verification Record | Yes | Never |
| Applies | Every time | When no clause settles the question |

A statement that must hold every time belongs in a [clause](../primitives/). "No payout above $10,000 without review" has no "when in doubt" in it. It is a constraint.
