## One method, many settings

A team building billing rules and a team building robot safety software should not give agents the same freedom. Most methods force a choice. Write the process for the cautious team and slow everyone down, or write it for the fast team and exclude the careful ones.

CanonBall keeps the lifecycle the same everywhere and varies one thing: a level, kept per kind of decision, that says how much of that decision an agent may take.

## Six levels of automation

Human-factors researchers have described the handover from operator to machine as a scale of *levels of automation* since the 1970s. This is a short version of that scale.

| Level | The agent | A person |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Analyzes | Decides |
| 1 | Recommends, with reasons and stated confidence | Decides |
| 2 | Decides | Approves before anything happens |
| 3 | Acts | Reviews afterwards, every time |
| 4 | Acts | Audits a sample |
| 5 | Acts | Watches the metrics |

A new team starts every kind of decision at level 1. Agents recommend. People decide.

## Classify the decision, not the topic

A decision is classified by three properties.

| Property | The question |
|---|---|
| **Reversibility** | What would it cost to undo, and who would have to agree? |
| **Blast radius** | How much breaks if it is wrong? |
| **Verifiability** | Can a machine check the result? |

These give a small number of classes, from "reversible, local, checkable" to "irreversible, broad, uncheckable". The class decides where the level starts. The subject matter does not.

That explains why domains differ. A billing service has many decisions in the first class. A robot fleet has many in the last. Each ends up with different levels without anyone arguing about the method.

## How a level moves

| Direction | What moves it | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Up** | Track record | At level 1 the agent runs in shadow mode: it records what it would have decided. Enough agreement with the person, over enough cases, moves the class up one level. |
| **Down** | One failure | A single escaped defect drops that class one level. |
| **Neither** | A model release | A better model usually earns the move faster. It still has to earn it. |

The thresholds are written in advance in the policy, which no agent can edit. For example: "promote after 50 cases with at least 98% agreement and no severe disagreement."

A level works like a credit score. It is earned slowly and lost quickly.

## What never moves

Two kinds of decision stay with people at every level.

- **What the system promises.** Agents may draft promises and examples. People approve them. A machine cannot verify meaning, so meaning cannot be delegated.
- **What is never allowed.** Constraints have their own owner, with a veto.

## How decisions connect to design

"How much of the design should agents own?" is the question teams argue about most. CanonBall turns it into three small controls.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **The clause state** | A clause is *decided*, *deferred* to the agents within bounds, or *open*. Deferring a clause hands the choice over. |
| **Promotion** | Any design choice an agent recorded in the blueprint can be promoted to a decision in the canon. From then on a person owns it. |
| **The level for the class** | Low-level design in a well-understood domain can reach level 4 or 5. High-level design in a new domain may stay at level 1 for a long time. |

Whatever the level, every decision still traces to a clause, and every clause to a promise. The level changes who decides. It never changes what a decision must answer to.

## Precedents carry judgment

When an agent meets a case no clause covers, it asks. The person's answer is recorded as a **precedent**: the case, the ruling, and the reason.

> Order 4471, a bundle with one late item: refund the late item only, because the rest delivered value.

The agent applies precedents to similar cases and asks again when a case is materially different. When several precedents point the same way, the agent proposes a general rule, and a person approves it as a decision.

Delegation therefore grows as the body of precedent grows. A short ranked list of values covers the one thing precedents cannot: which goal wins when two conflict.

## What is unproven

Agreement measures imitation, and imitation is only as good as the person imitated. An agent that matches a person 98% of the time is as right as that person was. For classes where a wrong decision is severe, set the threshold higher. Some classes are so rare that they will never reach fifty cases, and those stay with people.
