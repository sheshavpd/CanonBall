## The four layers

<Figure id="layers" />

Canon and blueprint together are the **spec**, the statement of intent, and build and Verification Record together are reality. The agents' job is to keep reality consistent with intent and to say so when they cannot.

Read down the left side, each layer constrains the one below: the canon bounds what the blueprint may design, and the blueprint bounds what the build may contain.

Read up the right side, each layer answers to the one above. The build produces evidence. The blueprint holds, for each clause, an **assurance case**: the argument for how that clause will be shown true. The evidence discharges the cases, and the cases discharge the clauses in the canon.

When two layers disagree, the higher one is right by definition, and the change is blocked until they agree.

## Canon

The canon is the set of approved [clauses](../primitives/), plus a few pages that give them context: the purpose of the system, who it serves, the [vocabulary](../vocabulary/), and the [tenets](../tenets/), which say which good thing wins when two conflict.

It is written in plain language, and it is the only layer people must read in full. They read the others by exception.

## Blueprint

The blueprint is where design choices are written down, so that they are visible and can be reversed. It holds:

- the architecture and the contracts between internal modules;
- **derived decisions**, which are design choices an agent made and recorded with its reasons;
- one case per clause;
- the evidence plan and the design of each verification environment.

The canon leaves gaps on purpose, and the blueprint records how they were filled. Agents may revise it as often as they like, on the condition that it never contradicts the canon.

A person can move a derived decision up into the canon at any time, which is how a design choice is taken back from the agents: once promoted to a [decision](../primitives/#decision), it changes only when a person changes it. A team can also keep parts of the design with a person from the start, a choice that [Handing over decisions](../delegation/) covers.

## Build

The build has two halves. `src/` is the code that runs, written by the builder agent at the [Build](../build/) station. `verify/` is everything that produces evidence: environments, checks, injected faults, proofs and monitors, written by the verifier agent at the [Verify](../verify/) station.

The agent that writes `src/` can run what is in `verify/` and cannot change it, for reasons that [People and agents](../people-and-agents/) gives.

Both agents work by looking inside the running system. Beside the two halves sits `observe/`, which lists what the system emits and holds the tools that read it, as [Observability](../observability/) describes.

The build is replaceable, with one restriction: it may not be regenerated from the spec. The spec leaves many things open, and a fresh generation would decide each of them again, differently. The build therefore changes only as a sequence of checked changes.

## Verification Record

The Record states, for every clause, whether the evidence supports it. A program called the orchestrator computes it from the cases and the results of the checks, according to rules that people own.

Nobody writes "done" or "verified" into the Record, because it is not a file in the repository: it is recomputed on every push and published. The [release rule](../release/) is stated in terms of it.

## Two rules that make it mechanical

The layers are enforced by two automated checks, which run on every change.

> **Traceability.** Every element of the build refers to an element of the blueprint, and every element of the blueprint refers to a clause. Anything that cannot be traced blocks the merge.

This check establishes only that the references exist. Whether the code is justified by them is the job of the case.

> **No silent gaps.** No clause is reported as accepted while any part of its case lacks evidence. Anything that has not been assessed is shown as unknown and is never left out.

Both rules are computed, and neither involves a person.
