# INC-007  Refund refused on day 29

Detected:     a support report. The customer of order 5512 asked for a refund on 2 April 2026, 29 days after
              delivery, and was refused as too late. No monitor fired: none watches refusals.
Diagnosis:    the on-call agent read the decision log for the order (below). The REQ-012 decision was
              "day 31 of 30", counted from dispatch on 2 March 2026. Delivery was 4 March 2026.
Cause:        the refund window was counted from the day of dispatch, where REQ-012 says delivery.
Repair:       inc-007-window-from-delivery (roadmap/unplanned/ until released, then roadmap/archive/)
Example added: REQ-012 satisfies/2. Given an order dispatched on 2 March and delivered on 4 March, when
              the customer requests a refund on 2 April, then the request is accepted.
Proposed:     INV-009 The refund window is counted from delivery, never from dispatch.
Environment:  VE-002 could always set up an order whose dispatch and delivery days differ, but no check
              used one: every order it set up was delivered on the day of dispatch. The repair adds late
              delivery to its starting states, and checks that start from it.

## What the decision log showed

```text
$ observe/tools/observe decisions --order 5512
REQ-012  refund window
  result        refused
  reason        day 31 of 30
  counted from  dispatch, 2 March
  delivered     4 March
```

Order 5512 was dispatched on 2 March and delivered on 4 March. The request on 2 April is day 29 counted from
delivery, inside the window, and day 31 counted from dispatch, outside it. The decision logic read the day
of dispatch (`window_start` in `src/refunds/decide.py`). The vocabulary defines delivery as the carrier's
delivered scan, not the dispatch.

## Why the Verification Record showed REQ-012 as accepted

REQ-012 was accepted in CanonBall 12 and in CanonBall 13, and it was accepted the whole time the defect was
running. Both of its checks passed, and both had been seen to fail. But both used an order whose dispatch and
delivery fell on the same day, so counting from either day gave the same answer. Accepted means supported by
the evidence so far, and the evidence had a gap: no example separated dispatch from delivery. The repair adds
that example, and INV-009 makes the rule explicit so that a check fails if the window is ever counted from
dispatch again.

## What people decided

- The operator approved paying this customer by hand: money moving outside the normal path is a person's
  decision.
- The repair restores what REQ-012 already promised, so it takes the express belt in `roadmap/unplanned/`.
  The product manager approves the new example and INV-009.
